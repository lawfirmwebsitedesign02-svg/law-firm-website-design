# What Makes a Good Law Firm Website? A Practical Checklist

Most people choosing a lawyer never phone the firm first. They look at the website, and within seconds they decide whether this firm feels credible, approachable and right for their problem. A good law firm website doesn't just look professional. It answers questions, builds trust and makes the next step easy.

Use this checklist to see how your site measures up. Each section explains what to look for and why it matters.

## 1. Clear message above the fold

Visitors should know who you help, what you do and where you practise within about five seconds. "Family lawyers helping Adelaide parents reach fair, practical outcomes" says far more than "Trusted legal advice." Pair that message with one obvious call to action, such as "Book a consultation."

**Check:** Could a stranger describe your firm after a quick glance at the homepage?

## 2. Easy ways to make contact

Someone with a legal problem is often anxious and short on time. Put your phone number in the header, make it tap-to-call on mobile, and offer a short enquiry form and, if possible, online booking. Ask only for the essentials: name, phone, email and a brief description of the matter.

**Check:** Can a visitor contact you within two taps or clicks from any page?

## 3. Mobile-friendly design

Many people search for lawyers on their phones. Text should be readable without zooming, buttons should be easy to tap and pages should load quickly on a mobile connection. Good [**law firm website design**](https://lawfirmwebsitedesign.com.au/) treats mobile as the main experience rather than a shrunken version of the desktop site.

**Check:** Try to submit an enquiry on your own phone. Note anything that frustrates you.

## 4. Dedicated practice area pages

A single services page rarely convinces anyone. Each core practice area needs its own page covering what the matter involves, the usual process, typical timeframes, common questions and what to do first. These pages also match specific searches, which is central to **SEO for lawyers**.

**Check:** Does each major service have a page that a client with that problem would find genuinely helpful?

## 5. Plain-English content

Clients aren't lawyers. Dense paragraphs and unexplained terminology make people feel out of their depth. Use short paragraphs, clear headings and simple explanations, and define any technical term you can't avoid.

**Check:** Would a non-lawyer understand your main practice pages on a first read?

## 6. Real people and real credibility

People hire people. Include professional photos, short bios, areas of focus, admissions, accreditations and years in practice. A warm, direct introduction to each lawyer builds more trust than a list of credentials alone.

**Check:** Can a visitor see who they'd be working with and why they're qualified?

## 7. Compliant trust signals

Awards, memberships and client feedback are useful, but Australian legal advertising is regulated. Rules under the Legal Profession Uniform Law and state and territory regulators restrict misleading or deceptive claims, and testimonials and results need particular care. Check your local law society's guidance before publishing anything that suggests outcomes.

**Check:** Has a lawyer at the firm reviewed every claim on the site against the advertising rules?

## 8. Transparency on cost and process

Fear of unknown costs stops many people from calling. You don't have to publish a full fee schedule, but you can explain how you charge, whether you offer fixed fees or an initial consultation, and what affects the final cost. Outlining what happens after someone enquires makes the first step feel manageable.

**Check:** Does your site answer "What will this cost, and what happens next?"

## 9. Solid on-page SEO

Each page needs a unique title tag and meta description, one clear H1, logical subheadings and descriptive image alt text. Use your main topic and location naturally in headings and copy, and link related pages together. Avoid keyword stuffing, since it hurts readability and rankings.

**Check:** Does every important page target one clear topic and location?

## 10. Fast, secure and technically healthy

Slow pages lose visitors and rankings. Compress images, use reliable hosting and monitor Core Web Vitals in Google Search Console. Run the whole site on HTTPS, keep your CMS and plugins updated, and fix broken links and forms promptly.

**Check:** Does your site pass Core Web Vitals on mobile?

## 11. Strong local signals

Most clients come from a defined area. Keep your Google Business Profile accurate, use consistent name, address and phone details across directories, and create a page for each office with a map and transport or parking details. Local relevance also matters for AI search tools, which look for clear jurisdiction and location information.

**Check:** Would Google and a potential client both know exactly where you operate?

## 12. Accessibility and privacy

A good site works for everyone. Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA: strong colour contrast, keyboard navigation, captioned video and descriptive form labels. Because clients share sensitive information, explain how you handle personal data and keep your privacy policy aligned with the Privacy Act and your professional obligations.

**Check:** Can someone using a screen reader or keyboard complete your enquiry form?

## 13. Fresh, useful content

A blog or resource hub that answers real questions shows expertise and attracts people early in their decision. Focus on local, state-specific topics, add "last reviewed" dates to legal content and update pages when the law changes. Outdated information damages trust with clients and search engines alike.

**Check:** Have your key legal pages been reviewed in the last 12 months?

## 14. Measurable results

A good website is tracked. Set up analytics for calls, form submissions and booking clicks, and record where each enquiry comes from. Review the data monthly and improve pages that get traffic but no enquiries.

**Check:** Do you know which pages and sources produce your consultations?

## Quick scoring guide

Give yourself one point for each checklist item you can confidently tick off.

- **12 to 14:** Your site is in strong shape. Focus on content and continual testing.
- **8 to 11:** You have a solid base with clear gaps. Fix contact options, practice pages and mobile experience first.
- **7 or below:** Consider a structured review or redesign, starting with your message, contact paths and core practice area pages.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What makes a good law firm website?**
A good site communicates clearly who you help, builds trust with real people and credentials, works well on mobile, explains your services in plain English and makes contacting you simple. It's also fast, secure, accessible and compliant with advertising rules.

**How many pages should a law firm website have?**
There's no fixed number, but most firms need a homepage, a page for each core practice area, team profiles, a contact page and a privacy policy. Add location pages, FAQs and a resource section as you grow.

**Does law firm website design affect SEO?**
Yes. Structure, speed, mobile usability, headings and internal linking all influence search performance. Design and [**SEO for lawyers**](https://lawfirmwebsitedesign.com.au/seo-for-lawyers/) work best when planned together rather than separately.

**Should a law firm website include client testimonials?**
They can help, but rules differ across Australian states and territories. Check your regulator's guidance before publishing testimonials or outcome claims, and avoid anything that could mislead.

**How often should I update my law firm website?**
Review core pages at least quarterly to keep team details, contact information and legal content accurate. Publish new content regularly, ideally monthly, and update older articles when the law changes.

**Do I need a redesign or just improvements?**
If the site is structurally sound, targeted improvements to messaging, contact options and practice pages may be enough. If it's outdated, hard to manage or poor on mobile, a redesign is usually the better investment.

## Final thoughts

A good law firm website is clear, credible, easy to use and built around the questions real clients are asking. Work through the checklist, fix the biggest gaps first and track what happens. Small, steady improvements to your **law firm website** often produce more enquiries than any single big change.
